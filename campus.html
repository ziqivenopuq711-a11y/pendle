Our Campus

Tranquil spaces designed for comfort, connection, and a daily relationship with the landscape of Pendle Hill.

Setting and Grounds

The Pendle Hill Retirement campus occupies a sheltered south-facing slope at the foot of the hill, roughly two miles from the market town of Clitheroe. The grounds extend to five acres of managed gardens, wildflower meadows, orchard, and woodland edge, with direct footpath access onto the open moorland above. From almost any point on the campus, the broad silhouette of the hill is visible, offering an ever-present sense of orientation and calm.

The landscape was designed in close consultation with residents and a specialist horticultural therapist, with accessibility, sensory richness, and ecological diversity as equal priorities. Paths are wide, level-graded, and surfaced for year-round use. Raised beds bring the kitchen garden to a height comfortable for those who prefer to garden sitting down.

Residential Cottages and Apartments

Accommodation at Pendle Hill Retirement is arranged in a gentle arc of single-storey limestone cottages and a central two-storey building housing additional apartments. Each cottage contains a private bedroom, bathroom, sitting room with bay window, and a small private terrace or garden patch. Internal décor has been kept natural and unhurried — warm oak floors, cream walls, linen curtains — so that the view through the window is always the first thing the eye finds.

"When I opened my curtains on the first morning and saw the hill in the dawn light, I knew I was home." — A resident, arrived 2023

All residences are fully accessible, with thoughtfully integrated assistive features that do not interrupt the domestic character of the space. Emergency call systems, adjustable lighting, and easy-access wet rooms are standard throughout.

Communal Spaces

The heart of our campus is the Common Room — a bright, generously proportioned gathering space at the centre of the main building, with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the garden and the hill beyond. Here, residents share meals, exchange stories, join workshops, listen to visiting musicians, and enjoy long quiet afternoons with a book and a pot of tea.

Adjacent to the Common Room is the Dining Room, where three meals a day are prepared from fresh, largely locally sourced ingredients, including produce from our own kitchen garden. The menus change seasonally and are shaped each month by a resident food committee. Dietary needs, preferences, and celebrations are all accommodated with pleasure.

A smaller Library and Quiet Room sits at the eastern end of the building, stocked with books on natural history, local history, literature, and Quaker thought, reflecting the intellectual and contemplative traditions of the place. A weekly reading group, a poetry corner, and a writing group all find their home here.

The Kitchen Garden and Orchard

Our walled kitchen garden produces vegetables, herbs, and soft fruit across a dozen raised and in-ground beds, tended by residents and a part-time horticulturalist. The garden is a working, productive space but also one of the most popular spots for quiet contemplation on the campus. Seasons are marked here very clearly: the first rhubarb of spring, the glut of courgettes in summer, the apple harvest in September, the satisfying bare earth of November.

The orchard beyond the garden contains heritage Lancashire varieties of apple, pear, and plum. Residents are involved in the annual harvest, and the resulting preserves, juices, and crumbles find their way onto the dining room table through the winter months.

Moorland Access and Walking

One of the distinctive gifts of this campus is its direct connection to open moorland. A gate at the northern edge of the grounds opens onto a public footpath that winds upward through bracken and heather to the summit of Pendle Hill. Our staff lead optional guided walks at varying paces each week, from a gentle 20-minute stroll along the lower paths to the full summit ascent for those who wish it. The experience of standing on the high ridge with the wind in one's hair and the whole of Lancashire spread below is, by all accounts, irreplaceable.

For residents who prefer to receive the moor rather than walk it, there are several sheltered seating areas along the garden boundaries where the sounds, scents, and skies of the upland come freely and without effort.
